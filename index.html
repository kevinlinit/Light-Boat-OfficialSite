How a trip comes together

  1. Tell us how you travel

    A fifteen-minute questionnaire: your pace, early mornings or late nights, hot springs and tattoos, food and allergies, fears, what you've seen already, and your budget.

  2. Get route ideas from a planner

    A planner reads every answer and writes to you with a route that fits: where you'll sleep, how long you stay, and why it suits you. You change what you like.

  3. Your trip arrives in the app

    Sign in to Mission Timeline with the same email and the itinerary is there, day by day, with the times that can't move, notes for every stop, and a Plan B for rain. When your planner updates it, your copy updates too.

  4. Travel with it

    The app shows what's happening now and what's next, with reminders before the moments that matter. Mark what you did, skipped or changed, and your planner sees it and adjusts the rest of the trip.

Where trips often go

Every trip is built for one traveller or group, but these are routes we often start from.

  • Kyushu: volcanoes and hot springs

    Fukuoka, Beppu, Kurokawa Onsen, Aso

    Steaming spa towns, a living volcano and slow ryokan nights, ending in Fukuoka's food stalls.

  • The Kumano pilgrimage

    Koyasan, the Kumano Kodo, Nachi, Shirahama

    A temple night on Koyasan, then forest trails between grand shrines and riverside hot springs.

  • The Japan Alps and old post towns

    Matsumoto, Kamikochi, Takayama, Shirakawa-go, Kanazawa

    Alpine valleys, a thatched-roof village and the merchant streets of Takayama and Kanazawa.

  • Setouchi: art islands and the inland sea

    Kurashiki, Naoshima, Teshima, Onomichi, Hiroshima

    Museums set into island hills, ferry hops between fishing towns and a bike ride over the sea.

  • Tohoku: the deep north

    Matsushima, Hiraizumi, Ginzan Onsen, Yamadera, Kakunodate

    Pine islands, golden temples, a gaslit onsen town and samurai lanes, far from the crowds.

  • San'in: Japan's quiet sea coast

    Izumo, Matsue, Tottori, Kinosaki Onsen

    The gods' shrine at Izumo, a castle town on a lake, sand dunes, and an onsen town made for bath-hopping.
